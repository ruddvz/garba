THE GARBO

The dance moves around a devotional centre.

During Navratri, Garba can take place around an image of Amba or a perforated earthen pot lit with an oil lamp. The dancers move around that shared devotional centre.

01 · THE CENTRE

The centre is devotional, not stage decoration.

UNESCO describes Garba dancers moving around either an image of the mother goddess Amba or a perforated earthenware pot lit with an oil lamp. The illuminated pot is commonly referred to as a garbha-deep.

This matters because the circle is not organised around empty space. The shared focus at the centre is part of the ritual structure of Garba during Navratri.

An image of Amba can occupy that role too. The lit pot is one recognised form of the centre, not a requirement for every Garba gathering.

02 · THE CIRCLE

The dancers move in relation to the centre.

UNESCO describes dancers moving around the centre in a counter-clockwise circle, using simple movements while singing and clapping in unison. The tempo can build from slower circular movement into faster whirling.

That makes the relationship easy to read: centre, circle, people and rhythm belong to the same practice.

03 · THE POT

Light shines from inside the earthen form.

The form UNESCO describes is perforated earthenware with an oil lamp inside. Shapes, decoration and local customs can vary, so it is better understood as a living craft practice than as one fixed object design.

PlayGarba uses the lit centre carefully in its cultural and visual work because it carries meaning beyond decoration.

04 · COMMUNITY

The centre is made and carried by a wider community.

UNESCO's description of Garba includes dancers, musicians, social groups, craftspeople and religious figures among the practitioners and bearers of the tradition.

Pottery, clothing, ornaments, music and dance are connected parts of the same living practice. That is why understanding Garba requires more than identifying a song or a dance step.
