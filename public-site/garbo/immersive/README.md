# The Immersive player

PlayGarba's Immersive player: the Garbo player whose thirteen venues (Outdoors, Stadium, Sheri, Obsidian Edge, Resham Dome, Chitra Aangan, Voltage Lab, Chandra Van, Vrindavan Courtyard, Tulip Grove, Lotus Amphitheatre, Vadodara Vision 2047 and Jyot Chowk) are built in 3D. Immersive on the main site loads this page with `?live=1&embed=1`, and the production player stays the only playback owner. The prototypes it grew from live in `../prototypes/` (`2d/` is the 2D Garbo, `3d/` a frozen copy of this page to experiment on). Issues #2003, #2112, #2152 and #2160. A first visit opens in Pandora.

Open `/garbo/immersive/` on its own (add `#outdoors`, `#stadium`, `#sheri`, `#pandora`, `#resham`, `#chitra`, `#voltage`, `#chandra`, `#vrindavan`, `#tulip`, `#lotus`, `#vadodara`, `#jyot`, `#tideglass`, `#shikhar` or `#kutch` to pick a venue, and `?venue=2d` to compare with the 2D venue). Each venue also has a clean link of its own, one lowercase word: `/garbo/immersive/obsidian/` (and `pandora`, `outdoors`, `stadium`, `sheri`, `resham`, `chitra`, `voltage`, `chandra`, `vrindavan`, `tulip`, `lotus`, `vadodara`, `jyot`, `tideglass`, `shikhar`, `kutch`), which opens the player there; add `#far` to open it from far off. (Obsidian Edge, Resham Dome and Voltage Lab keep their first ids, `pandora`, `resham` and `voltage`, so saved links still work.) The old `/garbo/prototype-3d/` link forwards here.

## How it's split

- **The people stay 2D.** `venue-scene.js` is a copy of the production venue scene (`public-site/atmosphere/scene.js`) with a backdrop hook. It draws everything that lives and moves: the dancers, the band, the vendors and the DJ, the screens' pictures (drone feed, singer close-ups, the aarti screen) and the effects.
- **Everything that stays put is 3D.** `venue3d/venue3d.js` draws, in WebGL under the 2D canvas, the sky, ground, stage and its gear, towers, stands, shamiana, houses, trees, chhatris, bulb strings, lanterns and beams, the garbo, and (`venue3d/src/furnish.js`) the food stalls and the pani puri cart, the DJ's whole rig, chairs, benches, parked scooters, bikes and the van, tulsi planters and tents. It uses the 2D scene's own camera each frame, so the people stand exactly on the 3D ground.
- **One file per venue.** Each 3D venue is its own module (`venue3d/src/venues/<id>.js`), built into a chunk of its own: the shared core (three.js, the kit, the lighting, the backdrop) loads with the page, and a venue's chunk is fetched only when that venue is wanted. Once you're in a venue, its neighbours in the list are built in the background up to what the device holds (desktop 3, tablet 2, phone 1), and the one left longest ago is let go. Furniture and dressing more than one venue uses (lanterns, sofas, tables, rugs, urns of dandiya sticks, palms, bells, truss, spreading trees with their leaves, arches of branches) are in `venue3d/src/venues/decor.js`; shared building blocks (faces with light baked into stone, noise) in `venues/common.js`.
- **Each new venue describes itself.** From Pandora on, a venue's 2D side is a spec in `venues2d/<id>.js` (loaded by `index.html` before the scene): its cameras and framing, the floor people dance on and where you can walk, the band's stage, the DJ, where people sit (from far off, between songs, round the floor), its drone airspace, its sound, its sky and how it's drawn without WebGL2. The scene reads the spec; it has no venue-specific code for them. The 3D build is handed the same spec (`data.spec`), so the sofas it builds are the ones the people sit on. To add a venue: a `venues2d/<id>.js`, a `venue3d/src/venues/<id>.js`, and one line in `venue3d/src/venue.js`.
- **Walking.** On a laptop the arrow keys (or WASD) walk the two of you anywhere you can stand, in any direction: each key is taken against the view as you press it, so ↓ turns you round and walks you back, ← and → turn you aside, and the view swings round to follow from behind you. On a touch screen, with the player hidden, a drag steers the same way. Where you can't go you slide along the edge; walking up to the band stands you by the stage, walking up to the DJ opens his laptop to search for a song, and Escape takes you home to the circle.
- **The player.** Only the song's title, its singers, the progress bar and the controls, with the controls at the bottom. The top bar is the same in every venue and on every screen: the home button where the moon was turns, in place, into two (home goes to Simple, full screen toggles full screen), and View, Sound and Ideas sit directly under More. Tonight and Play YouTube link are in More. There is no genre strip and no Explore: Search walks you to the DJ, whose laptop is where you choose. (The production player keeps its genre strip and Explore; this is the 3D page's own player.)
- **Every panel opens as words over the venue** (View, Sound, Ask for a feature, More and everything in it, What is Garba, and in the Immersive view the site's own Ask Kukdu and Private Garba Circle): no card, the venue shaded behind, the player stepping back while it's open (the song plays on), and the name tags, the walking hint and the site's start hint stepping back too. Each is a column centred on the screen on a soft shade of its own: a serif title with its close button on the line, labels in brass, choices as words with a line under the one you're on, an input as a line to write on, one brass button for the action. On a phone the column is the width of the screen, below the View, Sound and Ideas pill, and scrolls up and down only; View's venues are three to a line there, More's doors too. On a wide screen View is three columns, each centred in its own space and each on a faint shade of its own so it reads over a busy venue: down the left, how you dance (a woman or a man, with your partner or alone, each an icon over its word), where you stand, and your names and faces; in the middle of the screen a map of the venue drawn in light: tap anywhere on it (a seat, a step, the floor, the stage or the DJ) and you're there, any seat, not only the set ones; on the right the venues by name, each with its own line icon, the finished ones first and the ones still being built under a heading of their own. On a phone it's one column: venues two to a line, you, the map, where you stand, names. While a venue is being built its name turns and the map says so. The address follows the venue you're in.
- **Venues still being built** (a spec with `soon: true`) can be danced in already; the first time you're in one in a visit, a signboard with hazard stripes swings down under the top bar to say it opens fully very soon.
- **Screens in the air.** Obsidian Edge has two translucent screens of light floating over its terraces. Resham Dome, Tulip Grove and Lotus Amphitheatre each have two sheets of nearly clear glass, each carried on tethers by a pair of small drones (`droneScreen` in `venue3d/src/venues/common.js`), showing the sponsors. Voltage Lab and Vadodara show theirs on their LED walls; Chandra Van, Chitra, Vrindavan and Jyot Chowk have no screens.
- **Aerial.** Every venue has a drone's view: View → Where you stand → Aerial, or `#<venue>-aerial` (a clean link takes `#aerial`). The view circles slowly round the floor, high up, looking in at it, as the references' aerial shots do (the sheri's lane swings to and fro instead; the stadium and Voltage Lab keep under their roofs). The sound is the far-away mix. Each venue sets its own height and radius (`aerialCam` in its spec); from the air the haze thins and a wide land runs out to the horizon in the venue's own tone.
- **Keys.** Tab moves to the next view within the venue (from the circle, far off, the stage, the air), Shift+Tab to the next venue; F6 moves into the top bar for keyboard navigation. Tab only switches views while nothing on the page has focus, so it never traps a keyboard user in a form or a sheet.
- **Explore anywhere.** Every venue can be walked end to end: the floor, the seats and lounges, up steps, terraces and tiers (each spec's `heightAt` lifts the two of you and the view as you climb), through gates and gardens, round the stage.
- **Ask Kukdu and the cards.** The rooster launcher sits between 24/7 Live and the YouTube credit (above the credit on a wide screen), and Ask Kukdu is also in More. Inside the player the page round this one opens Kukdu, as the 2D Garbo does; on its own this page loads Kukdu itself. Every card that opens over the venue (the sheets, the side cards, More and Kukdu's panel) shares one look: deep ink glass, a brass rim and a faint glow along the top.
- **The stage.** The outdoor and stadium stages are deeper and wider, with the LED screen running full height from the riser's floor behind the band to just under the truss, so the players stand in front of a big picture. Six players have their own places on the riser: tabla on a gaddi, dhol, guitar, drums on their own riser, keyboard and bass (four on the sheri's takht). Their fixed gear is 3D (`venue3d/src/band.js`): the drum kit on its riser, the keyboard on its X-stand (two tiers on a big stage) with the octapad beside it and a tablet of lyrics on a gooseneck, the tabla on its gaddi, a boom mic for the dhol, amps, a spare guitar, a monitor wedge before every standing player and two before the singers (cut out of the singers standing behind them), a bottle of water and a taped set list by each player, cables across the boards to a stage box at the back, par cans washing the back of the stage in the venue's colour, and a soft pool of stage light on each player that goes down between songs. What they hold is drawn in 2D. The plan is `BAND` in both `venue-scene.js` and `venue3d/src/util.js`. In Pandora the band plays on a stone platform set into the far terraces.
- **The screens and the drone.** A 3D drone flies where the 2D scene's shot says (`venue3d/src/drone.js`), with spinning rotors, a gimbal camera and blinking navigation lights. Its aerial picture of the real 3D venue is rendered on the LED screen, and the 2D scene draws the shot's dancers over it with the same camera. The close shots (a child running through, the two of you, the lead singer) get the real venue behind them from a ground-level camera, softened. Phones keep the drawn map.
- **Sponsors.** The creatives are listed in `sponsors/sponsors.json` (BookPhysio's five, from #2023, to start): add, remove or change images there and every place uses them. The places are the stage screens' sponsor breaks, the outdoor side screens between singer close-ups, the five LED panels in the outdoor stage's skirt, the stadium's corner screens and the sheri's boards. No place shows a creative for more than five seconds: every five seconds each place takes a new one from a fresh shuffle, never the one it just showed, and places on show at the same moment never show the same one. The 2D scene plans this (`sponsorPlan`) and tells the 3D venue what each of its places shows. Each creative's white margin is trimmed off; screens and boards show it edge to edge, the skirt panels as a card on their maroon, and the 2D screens draw an enlarged, sharpened copy.
- **Floors.** Beaten earth, patterned cement tiles in deep reddish browns (as Athangudi tiles are made) for the hall, dark street stone (Kadappa-style slabs) for the lane, and polished obsidian with gold inlay and a star mandala for Pandora (`venue3d/src/floors.js`, Pandora's in its own module). Each floor also has a painted layer blended into the ground's shader: the trodden rings where each circle dances and a lime circle with marigold petals outdoors, a printed vinyl mandala in the stadium, and a powder rangoli in the sheri.
- **Far off is first person.** You sit among the people watching, just behind the two of you: their heads fill the bottom of the picture, round the player, and the dance floor the middle. Outdoors you're in the rows of chairs; in the stadium you sit in the near stand (built in 3D like the other three, `NSTAND` in both scripts), where each step hides the lower half of the people beyond it so you see heads and shoulders over the rows; in the sheri you're on a bench brought out on the lane, neighbours on plastic chairs beside you and children on the ground in front; in Pandora you sit on the third basalt step facing the band; in Resham, Chitra and Chandra on the second row of sofas or benches, looking over their backs; in Voltage Lab you stand at the near balcony's rail and look down over the floor.
- **The sheri.** House fronts with framed windows glowing through coloured curtains, carved doors standing open under torans with શુભ and લાભ either side (the leaves swung in, the lit front room beyond and its light spilling onto the doorstep), and curtain lights down most of them; a parked van with its seams, lamps, mirrors and a tarp-covered bundle on the roof rack; the haveli at the end of the lane with jharokhas, marigold swags and the society's projector screen; a canopy of bulb strings over the mandap and star kandils down the lane.

- **Both read one layout.** The 2D scene hands the backdrop its own layout objects (where each stall, chair and scooter is), so the two agree on every position.
- **Depth between the two.** A 3D thing someone can stand behind leaves the 2D scene its outline (convex solids on its layout object). At its place in the 2D scene's depth order, the 2D scene cuts that outline out of whatever it drew behind it, then draws the person at it, then cuts the parts in front of them (a counter, the DJ's table, a chair's back, the wedge monitors before the singers). So a stall hides the people behind it, and its counter hides the vendor's legs.
- **The way in: through the clouds.** Each visit opens above dense, moonlit clouds and stars while the venue builds below, with PlayGarba and "Tap anywhere to start the garba" over them (and "Setting up the venue" until it's built). The tap (or Enter) starts the music and drops you through the clouds: they rush up past you, the nearest swelling as you pass, and once the venue is built they part and the venue's own drop-in carries you down to your place. A slow device keeps you falling through cloud until its venue is ready, never more than a few seconds. Inside the site the tap is the site's own first tap, so the song starts as it always has; its own hint stays hidden while the gate is up, as do the venue's still and the under-construction board (which waits until you've landed). The clouds are five soft puffs drawn once and a half-resolution canvas, so a phone spends little on them. `?still` and `?gate=0` skip it (stills and checks).
- **Never a black screen.** From the page's first moment a still of the venue you're about to enter covers the screen (`venue-art/posters/<venue>-<stage|circle>[-tall].webp`, picked in `index.html` before anything else loads, wide on a laptop and tall on a phone), and it fades once the live venue is drawn; switching to a venue that isn't built yet shows its still too. The stills are frames of the real 3D venues, made by `venue3d/posters.mjs`: run it again after changing a venue.
- **It never shows the old venue.** The 3D script loads as an ES module. Until its first venue is built and its shaders are compiled (off the main thread), the scene shows the night the drone is waiting in (stars, slow clouds and the drone with its rotors and blinking lights), never a black frame, and never the 2D venue. Once the venue's first frame is drawn, the view drops in once per page: down through the clouds onto the outdoor ground, between the sheri's rooftops, or in through the stadium's carved doors as they swing open (straight in under reduced motion). Once the first venue is up, neighboring venues are built in the background, so switching venues is instant, and a switch cross-fades from the last frame. Without WebGL2, or if anything fails, the 2D venue simply draws instead.

## Lighting layers

Every light belongs to one layer, and each layer eases to the level the night calls for (paused, playing or an aarti). Each kind of source also has its own colour of light (`LIGHT` in `venue3d/src/util.js`), so a diya reads differently from a bulb or a floodlight even at the same brightness.

| Layer | What it is | Its light |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ambient | sky bounce and moonlight | cool |
| key | floodlights and the roof's key light (the one shadow caster) | cool white (about 4500 K) |
| architectural | uplights on the kanat and trees, wall washers, aisle and step lights, the wash on the temple spire | amber, steady |
| practical | street lamps, jhummars, lanterns, lit windows, door lamps, the stalls' tube lights and bulbs, house lights | warm bulbs (2700 K), amber sodium street lamps, cold tube lights |
| festive | bulb strings, fairy lights, curtain lights down the house fronts, star kandils, LED boards | the night's colours |
| show | stage washes, moving heads and their beams, the DJ's sign, pads, moving heads and LED strip | the night's colours |
| flame | diyas round the rangoli, on doorsteps, at the shrine and tulsi planters, on the DJ's table | orange (about 1900 K), each flame flickering on its own |
| garbo | the lamp in the pot, flickering with the scene's lit value | warm |

In an aarti the electric layers fall back and the flames rise, so the fire carries the light.

The picture is tone mapped with three.js's Neutral curve, which keeps each light's colour and rolls bright light off softly, and the bloom only picks up the lamps themselves.

Light that falls on the ground is painted once into one small light map per layer and blended by a single shader, so hundreds of lamps cost one draw. The flame layer's map flickers in the shader, differently from place to place. Surfaces lit by their own lamps (a stall's counter and valance, the stadium's steps) take a little of that light as their own glow, following their layer. See `venue3d/src/lighting.js`.

## Devices

`venue3d/src/backdrop.js` picks a tier: phones get a smaller pixel budget, no real-time shadows, fewer lights and a lighter bloom. On every tier, quality steps down on its own when frames run slow (fewer pixels, then no bloom, then a still venue redrawn on every other frame) and back up after several seconds of smooth frames; building a venue, a venue switch or a return to the tab don't count as slow.

A phone builds only the venue you're in and frees the one you leave; a tablet keeps two, a computer three (the one you're in and its neighbours, so switching to them is instant). On phones and tablets the venue is drawn only down to the player's controls, or above the DJ's laptop. The drone's picture on a stage screen is drawn at the screen's size on your screen, and refreshed half as often when it's small and far off.

Everything that never moves is baked (`venue3d/src/bake.js`): plain surfaces write their colour into their vertices and merge by finish, so a venue is a little over a hundred draws on a phone. Repeated things (bulbs, flames, chairs, scooters, beads) are instanced.

## Building

The bundle is committed, so the site needs no build step. After changing `venue3d/src/`, rebuild with esbuild and three.js r186 installed outside the repository:

```bash
mkdir -p /tmp/venue3d-tools && cd /tmp/venue3d-tools && npm init -y && npm i esbuild@0.25.10 three@0.186.1
```

```bash
node public-site/garbo/immersive/venue3d/build.mjs /tmp/venue3d-tools
```

The venue stills are remade with playwright-core in the same tools folder (`npm i playwright-core@1`), cwebp (`brew install webp`) and the repository served on a local port:

```bash
node public-site/garbo/immersive/venue3d/posters.mjs /tmp/venue3d-tools http://localhost:4360/public-site/garbo/immersive/
```

The singer portraits, face cutouts and sample data live in `../shared/`, which the main site's artist artwork and both prototypes use too.
