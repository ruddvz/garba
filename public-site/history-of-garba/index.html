HISTORY OF GARBA

The setting changes. The practice continues.

Garba is carried across generations through Navratri devotion, circular movement, music, craft and participation. Rural and urban settings can differ, but the practice is learned by taking part.

01 · RITUAL STRUCTURE

Start with the centre and the circle.

UNESCO describes Garba as a ritualistic and devotional dance performed during Navratri, around a lit earthenware pot or an image of the mother goddess Amba. Dancers move around the centre while singing and clapping in unison.

This gives a useful foundation for understanding Garba over time. The practice is not defined by one singer, one venue or one modern event format. Its identity is carried through collective participation around a devotional centre.

02 · ACROSS GENERATIONS

Garba is learned by doing it with other people.

UNESCO says Garba is transmitted in rural and urban areas through practice, performance, imitation and observation. That means continuity does not depend on freezing one version of the dance in time.

People watch, join, learn, perform and teach. The practice continues because it remains active rather than becoming only an object of historical study.

03 · CHANGING SETTINGS

Continuity does not require every Garba to look the same.

Garba is practised in both rural and urban settings. Contemporary gatherings may use larger grounds, amplification, recorded music, newer arrangements and changing dress alongside older community practices.

The useful question is not simply whether a setting looks old or new. It is whether the devotional centre, collective movement, music and community context remain understandable rather than being reduced to generic festival imagery.

04 · WHO CARRIES IT

The tradition includes more than dancers.

UNESCO describes the practitioners and bearers of Garba broadly, including dancers, musicians, social groups, craftspeople and religious figures involved in the festivities and preparations.

This wider view connects music, pottery, clothing, ornaments, dance and organisation. It also explains why PlayGarba's cultural pages separate the listening experience from the evidence-backed context around it.

05 · RECOGNITION

Garba of Gujarat entered UNESCO's Representative List in 2023.

UNESCO inscribed Garba of Gujarat on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2023, recognising it as living heritage carried by communities and transmitted across generations.
