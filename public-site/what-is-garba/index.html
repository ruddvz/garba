GARBA OF GUJARAT

What is Garba?

Garba is a ritualistic and devotional dance tradition from Gujarat, performed especially during Navratri. Dancers move together in a circle around a lit earthen pot, the garbha-deep, or an image of the mother goddess Amba.

01 · WHAT IS GARBA?

A devotional circle dance from Gujarat.

Garba is closely associated with Navratri and the worship of feminine energy, or Shakti. UNESCO describes the dance as taking place around a perforated earthenware pot lit with an oil lamp, or around an image of Amba.

Singing, clapping and instrumental accompaniment help hold the group rhythm while dancers move together around the centre.

02 · WHAT IS DANDIYA RAAS?

A related Raas form danced with sticks.

Dandiya is commonly used for forms of Raas in which dancers use small wooden sticks. Gujarat Tourism describes Dandiya/Raas alongside Garba during Navratri, while keeping the stick-based form distinct from Garba itself.

On the same festival night, people may move between Garba and Dandiya/Raas as the music and dance form change.

03 · GARBA VS DANDIYA

They belong together, but they are not the same dance.

Garba centres on collective circular movement, traditionally around the garbha-deep or Amba, and often uses clapping as part of the movement. Dandiya/Raas is a related form in which wooden sticks become part of the rhythm and choreography.

Calling every Navratri dance “Dandiya” misses that distinction. Garba, Raas and Dandiya can share a night without being interchangeable terms.

04 · WHAT MUSIC IS PLAYED DURING NAVRATRI?

The music follows the dance.

Navratri gatherings in Gujarat can move through sung Garba, instrumental accompaniment and Raas/Dandiya music. The sound changes with the form, but the music is built to support collective movement rather than sit apart from it.

05 · WHAT IS SANEDO?

A Gujarati narrative folk-song form.

Sanedo is a Gujarati narrative folk-song form, especially associated with North Gujarat. Gujarati Vishwakosh describes it as a humorous, dramatic narrative-song tradition with distinctive stories and folk tunes.

The name is traced to the Sanskrit word sneh, meaning affection. PlayGarba keeps Sanedo as its own listening category rather than treating it as another name for Garba.
