GARBA OF GUJARAT

A circle. A rhythm.
A living tradition.

Garba is a ritualistic and devotional dance tradition from Gujarat, performed especially during Navratri.

01 · THE CIRCLE

The centre matters.

Garba is closely associated with Navratri and the worship of feminine energy, or Shakti. UNESCO describes dancers moving in a circle around a lit earthen pot, the garbha-deep, or around an image of the mother goddess Amba.

The circle is both a visual form and a shared way of moving together.

02 · MUSIC AND MOVEMENT

The rhythm belongs to the group.

Singing, clapping and instrumental accompaniment connect the dancers to the beat. Garba can take place in neighbourhoods, villages, courtyards and large public grounds, but collective movement stays central.

03 · A LIVING TRADITION

Passed on by taking part.

Garba continues through families, performers, musicians, craftspeople, organisers and communities. Styles change, new music appears and gatherings grow, while the tradition remains recognisably Gujarati.

04 · UNESCO

Recognised in 2023.

UNESCO inscribed Garba of Gujarat on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2023.
