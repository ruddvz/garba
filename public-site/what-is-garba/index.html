GARBA OF GUJARAT

A circle. A rhythm.
A living tradition.

Garba is a ritualistic and devotional dance tradition from Gujarat, especially associated with Navratri. It is carried through music, movement, craft and community.

01 · WHAT GARBA IS

Devotion and community move together.

Garba is performed during Navratri, the Hindu festival associated with the worship of feminine energy, or Shakti. It brings dancers, singers, musicians, craftspeople, organisers and communities into the same cultural practice.

The form can be intimate or enormous. It can happen in a courtyard, a neighbourhood ground or a large public gathering. The scale changes, but collective movement remains central.

02 · THE CIRCLE

The centre matters.

UNESCO describes Garba as taking place around a perforated earthenware pot lit with an oil lamp, known as a garbha-deep, or around an image of the mother goddess Amba. Dancers move around the centre in a circle while singing and clapping in unison.

The circle is not just a graphic shape. It is one of the defining visual and communal structures of Garba.

03 · MUSIC AND MOVEMENT

The rhythm builds.

Garba can begin with simple, slower circular movements. As the music develops, the tempo increases and the movement becomes more energetic. Singing, clapping and instrumental accompaniment connect the dancers to the rhythm.

That relationship between music and movement is why PlayGarba treats the songs and the culture around them as part of the same story.

04 · A LIVING TRADITION

Passed on by doing.

Garba continues across generations through practice, performance, imitation and observation. The tradition is supported not only by dancers and musicians, but also by craftspeople who make garments, ornaments and the garbha-deep, alongside organisers and communities that sustain the gatherings.

It is not frozen in one visual style. Garba continues to change while retaining a recognisable cultural centre.

05 · UNESCO

Garba of Gujarat was inscribed in 2023.

In 2023, UNESCO inscribed Garba of Gujarat on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

For PlayGarba, that recognition reinforces why the catalogue should do more than play tracks. The people, context and tradition around the music deserve to remain discoverable too.
